# Public research website

The website tells the research story: the problem, the central idea, a concrete example, and a small set of results that make the idea tangible.

- Use reader-facing questions and conclusions to organize the page. Do not mirror the paper's experiment inventory.
- Keep evidence-version taxonomies, seed/run accounting, acceptance reports, raw-verification disclaimers, and internal audit prose out of the rendered website. Do not move the same material into visible footnotes or collapsible sections.
- Multi-seed robustness is supporting paper material, not a project-page storyline unless the author explicitly requests it.
- Retain only the metric labels and short comparison conditions needed to understand each chosen figure. A stronger story does not authorize changing values or overstating causal claims.
- Keep scientific source/version details in the shared data and internal checks. Do not couple provenance validation to visible source badges.
- Update the template and generator, not only index.html; test that report-style prose does not return.
- Keep the existing scientific identity, links, and exact released slide files unless their revision is separately requested.

## Public research talk

- The talk starts with title → roadmap → background: explain the memory need, the gap in existing work, and why CAMP is needed. Do not insert a method-specific case before those concepts are explained. Keep one complete case study after the results. A directory does not replace the motivation.
- Background follows memory need → tools available but underused → the hypothesis of a mismatch with pre-trained behavioral priors → the research question. Stop there: explain the file-memory solution and training mechanism in Method, and put the supporting interface-prior experiment in Results. The contribution overview follows Background as a separate preview. Bind tool-underuse claims to the actual comparison; do not imply every original method fails or that all workspace memory is absent.
- Preserve the actual contribution structure: CAMG environments, CAMG-RL learning method, and the empirical study. Use the original paper's core figures when they best explain the idea and method.
- Keep the roadmap short. Use lightweight chapter markers; group results by effectiveness, mechanism and transfer rather than listing every experiment. The main deck ends on takeaways, not an automatic appendix.
- Charts and values remain source-grounded. Keep detailed provenance, seed accounting and audit prose in the data and speaker notes, not audience-facing version badges.
- Rebuild both formats, notes and outline. Declare the editability boundary honestly: native text/charts/case diagrams, two paper-figure pictures in PPTX and original vectors in PDF.
